# Dinero Hwy — Go-To-Market (read this before any marketing work)

This is the strategy every page, ad, email, and pitch must serve. It overrides
clever ideas that don't fit it.

## The one truth: the work comes from the ground, in person
The only real wins so far came from **walking up and talking to owners directly**
— two taco stands, where value was added face-to-face. **In-person + local
networking is the primary sales channel.** Everything digital exists to support
that motion, not replace it.

## What the website is (and isn't)
- **IS:** credibility, a live demo, and **something valuable to bring to the
  in-person conversation** ("here's what I already built / graded for you").
- **IS:** promoted digitally as much as possible — reach, proof, portfolio.
- **IS NOT:** the closer. Do not architect or write the site as a cold-lead
  machine that's expected to sell on its own. It warms and validates; the human
  closes on the ground.

## ICP — who we actually target
**Approachable, owner-operated local businesses you can physically walk into and
talk to the owner.**
- **Yes:** food (taco stands, restaurants, butchers, bakeries), trades / home
  services (HVAC, plumbing, concrete, landscaping, roofing), auto (mechanics,
  detailing), shops, personal care (barbers, salons).
- **Owner profile:** 45+, "doesn't do computers," mastered the trade not the web,
  established with real word-of-mouth.
- **No (for now):** gated professional services — lawyers, doctors, offices with
  gatekeepers. Reaching them is a slower networking/referral game; deprioritize
  until the ground motion is proven and scaled.
- **Reach context (sourced):** ~36.2M US small businesses, ~27% with no website,
  ~52% of owners 55+, plus a large "has a weak site" segment. But **reach is gated
  by who we can reach in person** — the filter is *approachable local
  owner-operated*, not raw market size. (Nationally scalable later by productizing
  one niche.)

## Offer sequence — value first, automation second
1. **Create digital value first.** Get them online / build a real site / lock up
   their **digital real estate** — something tangibly better than what they have
   today. This is the door-opener and the trust-builder.
2. **Then streamline the bottleneck with AI.** Once they have the site and trust
   us, deepen the relationship by automating the choke point — missed calls, slow
   quotes, no follow-up. This is the expansion / higher-value work.

## The playbook (replicate the taco-stand motion)
1. Identify an approachable local owner (walk-in or local network).
2. Talk to the owner directly; understand the business.
3. Build something that **visibly helps** — often starting with the free Report
   Card or a quick real improvement they can see.
4. Deliver value first; earn trust.
5. Expand into the bottleneck-streamlining (AI) work.
6. Ask for the next introduction — networking compounds.

## Implications for how we build & write
- Site copy speaks like **proof you'd hand someone in person**, not a faceless SaaS.
- Lead with **getting online / digital real estate** (value), then reveal the
  **bottleneck-fix (AI)** as the deepening.
- Keep the free Report Card front-and-center — it's the perfect "value to bring to
  the conversation."
- Voice stays on the ethos in `messaging.md` (opportunity over fear, dignity,
  growth) and the honest price split ($12 land / site priced to fit).
