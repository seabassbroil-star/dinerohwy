# Discovery Playbook — PRIVATE (sales-side only)

> **Never referenced in public copy.** This is the across-the-counter method for learning a
> business's best customers and tuning the machine to find more of them. The public ads may
> gesture at "an untapped market" — the *method* below stays here.

## Why this works

An established business's past customers are the keys to its future ones. The owner already
knows who their best customers are — nobody has ever asked them the question and then *built
something* from the answer. That's the whole play: ask, listen, aim.

## The sit-down (15–20 minutes, after the Report Card has opened the door)

### 1. The keys — opening questions

Ask, in this order, and write the answers down on paper (paper matters — it's a working
session, not a pitch):

- "Who are your **ten best customers**? Names or types — whatever comes to mind."
- "How did **each one** find you?" (referral, drive-by, repeat, a sign, a friend…)
- "Which customers do you **wish you had more of**? Why those?"
- "Who calls you that you **wish wouldn't**?" (defines the negative filter — just as valuable)
- "When someone new calls, what do they usually **ask for first**?"

### 2. The commonalities grid

Sort the ten best onto a quick grid. Look for what repeats:

| Dimension | What to note |
|---|---|
| Who | homeowner / landlord / business / contractor / family |
| Where | neighborhoods, zip codes, radius from the shop |
| What | which service, ticket size, one-off vs repeat |
| When | season, day/night, emergency vs planned |
| Why them | speed? trust? price? bilingual? the only one who called back? |

Three or more matches on any dimension = a **pattern**. The pattern is the profile.

### 3. The lookalike profile

Write one sentence: *"Our best customer is a ___ in ___ who needs ___ and picks us because ___."*

That sentence becomes the machine's settings:
- **Site copy** leads with that need and that reason.
- **SEO / pages** target what that person actually searches.
- **Social pushes** go where that person actually looks (feed, maps, local groups).
- **Drip emails** speak to that job and that season.

### 4. The missed market

Because the business was never online, there is almost always a whole segment that has
**never heard of them** — people who search instead of ask a neighbor. Find it by asking:

- "Who never finds you today?" (new residents, younger owners, people outside the referral
  network, the other language — English or Spanish, commercial vs residential…)
- Which of their services gets **searched for** a lot but never rings their phone?

Name the segment out loud with the owner. Sizing it roughly ("every new homeowner in ___")
is enough — the point is they *see* it. This is the moment the $12 sign becomes obvious.

### 5. Tune the machine

Close the loop: "Everything we build gets aimed at these two groups — more of your best
customers, plus the ones who've never met you. And every piece is adjustable: if the calls
that come in aren't the right calls, we re-aim it."

That's why the operation is customizable at every point — it's tuned to *their* past
customers, not a template.

### 6. Leave-behind

End with the standing offer, in voice: "You know your business. We know how to put it
online. Twelve bucks a year for the sign — the rest we'll price to fit, and we'll tell you
straight what we can and can't do."
